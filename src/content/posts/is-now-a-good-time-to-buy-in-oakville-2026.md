If you have been thinking about moving to Oakville, the market you are looking at today is not the one that priced you out a few years ago. A recent comparison of Ontario markets puts Oakville's average home price about $529,100 below its 2022 peak, the largest dollar decline of any community in the comparison.

A number that size needs context. Oakville started from a much higher average than most Ontario towns, so the percentage drop is smaller than the dollar figure suggests, and the exact average moves month to month with what happens to sell. But a decline of more than half a million dollars tells you how much the buying environment has changed. For buyers who felt Oakville was out of reach in 2022, lower prices and a calmer market mean more choice, more room to negotiate, and a real chance to look at homes and neighbourhoods that did not fit the budget before.

## What you are buying into

The lower price is the opening. What you get for it is the reason to act.

- **Schools.** Oakville's reputation here is earned. In the Fraser Institute's latest rankings, Abbey Park and Iroquois Ridge secondary schools each scored 9.3 out of 10, tied for 8th in Ontario, and Forest Trail Public School scored a perfect 10. Rankings are one measure of a school, not the only one, but they explain why education is the first thing many families mention.
- **Waterfront and green space.** Lake Ontario shoreline, the harbours at [Bronte](/neighborhoods/bronte) and downtown, and more than 1,863 hectares of parkland connected by 255 kilometres of trails, by the Town's own count.
- **Established neighbourhoods.** From the tree-lined streets of [Old Oakville](/neighborhoods/old-oakville) to the family subdivisions of [River Oaks](/neighborhoods/river-oaks) and [West Oak Trails](/neighborhoods/west-oak-trails), each with its own price point and character.
- **The commute.** Oakville and Bronte GO stations, with express trains to Union in about 35 minutes, plus the QEW, 403 and 407 for everywhere else.
- **Everything else.** Recreation centres, the Oakville Centre for the Performing Arts, restaurants and shops downtown and in Kerr Village, and a hospital in town.

## Why the timing matters

Nobody can tell you where prices go next, and waiting for the exact bottom of a real estate market is a game most people lose. What you can see today is a substantial adjustment from peak pricing and a market where buyers have leverage they did not have three years ago.

That leverage shows up in ways beyond price. Conditions on financing and inspection are back in offers. Closing dates are negotiable. Sellers who need to move are listening to reasonable offers rather than waiting for a bidding war. Some of them need to sell now, and with fewer buyers competing, the opportunity is sitting there for the ones who are ready.

Ready means two things: a longer-term plan, so you are buying a home to live in rather than trading on a bounce, and the financial ability to move when the right house comes up. The second part starts with a [mortgage pre-approval](/blog/mortgage-pre-approval-explained-ontario-2026), which tells you your real budget and lets you write an offer with confidence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will Oakville prices fall further?**
Nobody knows, including anyone who tells you they do. What you can control is buying a home you can afford comfortably, at a price you have negotiated well, with a plan to stay long enough that short-term movement does not matter.

**Is Oakville still more expensive than Burlington and Mississauga?**
Yes, especially south of the QEW. The gap has narrowed in some segments. See the [three-city comparison](/blog/oakville-vs-burlington-vs-mississauga-buying-2026) for what each budget buys.

**Where do first-time buyers start in Oakville?**
Townhomes and condos in the north end and in Uptown Core are the usual entry point. The [First-Time Home Buyer Guide](/blog/first-time-home-buyer-guide-oakville-gta-2026) walks through the programs and the numbers.
